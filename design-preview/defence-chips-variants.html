Defence chips on the play sheet

Eleven shipped magic items grant a qualified defence ("+2 AC vs ranged attacks") or an incoming-attack statement ("attacks against you have disadvantage"). Both render in the builder and neither reaches the Combat screen, which has its own independent strip knowing four buckets. Each block below is the Combat strip — real tokens, the app's own chip CSS — at 1280 and again at 393. The labels are the ones agreed in review.

B — every group names what MOVES agreed

The Combat strip already answers the kinds question with a labelled group. What the labels sort on is whose die it is: AC and Saves move your own number or your own roll (a QualifiedDefense carries exactly that target), and Against you is the one group whose subject is somebody else — an IncomingAttack has no target at all, because the die is the attacker's.

The case goes in the CHIP, never in the label. An earlier pass called the first group "Only vs", which reads well until you notice that an incoming statement can be qualified too — Spellguard Shield is disadvantage:attacked.spell, equally "only vs spells". A scope is a qualifier, not a subject.

Saves is the open one. Two rows under one label, each a swing plus a case, is the part that has not found its form — everything else here is settled. Next variants start from this group.
